Term Definition
Physical activity Any movement of the body that uses energy, such as walking, sports, or exercise.
Nutrition The process of eating the right food to stay healthy and grow properly.
Mental health The health of a person's mind and feelings.
Sleep quality How well and how deeply a person sleeps, not just how many hours.
Screen time The amount of time a person spends using a phone, computer, or television.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 55-56: Struktur Generik dan Model Text

A. Generic Structure of Hortatory Exposition

Structure Function Typical Language Signal
Thesis Introduces the issue and signals that a call to action is coming. It is time to... / We need to talk about...
Arguments Gives reasons, often with data or facts, showing why the issue matters. Research shows that... / Firstly... / Furthermore...
Recommendation Gives a clear call to action that tells the reader exactly what to do. We should... / It is important that we... / Let us...

Key difference from analytical exposition (Unit 3): analytical exposition ENDS by repeating the writer's opinion (Reiteration). Hortatory exposition ENDS by telling the reader what action to take (Recommendation).

B. Model Text: "It's Time to Build Healthier Habits"

Thesis: It is time for teenagers to take their health seriously. Many students today spend hours on their phones, skip meals, and sleep far too little. These habits can seriously harm both their bodies and their minds.

Arguments: Firstly, research shows that teenagers need at least eight hours of sleep every night, but many students sleep less than six hours because of late-night screen time. This lack of sleep affects their focus, mood, and school results. Secondly, a poor diet with too much sugar and fast food can lead to low energy and make it hard to focus in class. Furthermore, spending too much time on screens and too little time being physically active can harm both physical fitness and mental health. It can make teenagers feel more stressed and worried.

Recommendation: Therefore, we should start building healthier habits today. We must aim for at least eight hours of sleep, reduce screen time before bed, eat more fruits and vegetables, and be physically active for at least thirty minutes a day. Small changes that we make every day can lead to a healthier and more energetic future.

LAMPIRAN 6 - MATERI AJAR (IN ENGLISH, LENGKAP DAN RINCI PER PERTEMUAN)

MATERI PERTEMUAN 57: Modal Verbs

Modal Verb Level of Obligation Example
Should A suggestion or advice, not a strict obligation. You should exercise regularly.
Must A strong obligation, with no choice. You must get enough sleep every night.
Need to A necessity, similar to must but slightly softer. We need to eat less sugar.

Grammar pattern: modal verb + bare infinitive (base form of the verb, no "to"). Example: "You should sleep early" (correct), not "You should to sleep early" (incorrect).

MATERI PERTEMUAN 58: Imperative Sentences

An imperative sentence gives a command, instruction, or invitation. It usually starts directly with the base form of the verb, without a subject (the subject "you" is hidden).

Example Function
Drink more water every day. A direct instruction.
Let's start building healthier habits today. An invitation using "Let's", softer than a direct command.
Don't skip breakfast. A negative imperative, telling someone what not to do.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 59-60: Logos, Pathos, Ethos

A. Three Persuasion Strategies

Strategy Meaning Example
Logos Persuasion through logic, data, and facts. "Research shows that teenagers need at least eight hours of sleep every night."
Pathos Persuasion through feelings and emotions. "Imagine feeling tired and unable to focus every day just because of poor sleep."
Ethos Persuasion through trust: an expert or a person we believe. "According to health experts, small daily habits make the biggest difference."

Note: a strong persuasive text often uses all three strategies, not just one. Using only pathos (emotion) without logos (data) may not convince people. Using only logos without any pathos can feel cold and far from our lives.

LAMPIRAN 6 - MATERI AJAR (IN ENGLISH, LENGKAP)

A. Criteria for Evaluating a Recommendation

Criterion Guiding Question
Relevance Does this recommendation matter to its audience (here, Indonesian teenagers)?
Feasibility Can the audience really do it with their daily schedule, time and money?
Cultural and Social Fit Does it fit the audience's culture, school system and social life?

Example evaluation: the recommendation "sleep at least eight hours every night" is very relevant, and the text supports it with research (logos). But it may not be feasible for students with a lot of homework or extracurricular activities. A more feasible version gives smaller, specific steps, such as going to bed at the same time every night or stopping screen time one hour before sleep.

LAMPIRAN 6 - MATERI AJAR: MENYUSUN SKRIP PODCAST (IN ENGLISH)

A. Podcast Script Structure

Section Role Content
Opening (Thesis) Host Greets the listeners and introduces the health topic.
Body (Arguments) Guest Stars / Narasumber Gives two or three strong reasons, with facts or data, about why this habit matters.
Closing (Recommendation) Host Gives a clear call to action and invites the listeners to change their habits.

B. Sample Script Excerpt (Model)

Host: "Hey everyone, welcome back to our show! Today, we need to talk about something that affects almost every teenager: sleep."

Guest Star: "That's right. Research shows that teenagers need at least eight hours of sleep, but many of us get much less because of late-night scrolling."

Host: "So, what should we do about it? Let's start small. Turn off your phone thirty minutes before bed, and go to sleep at the same time every night. You will feel the difference!"

Note: the speakers take turns, but the whole script argues for ONE clear recommendation, not two opposite sides. This keeps the podcast hortatory (persuasive, one direction). It is not a discussion text, which shows both sides of a debate and is a Kelas XII topic.

PAKET A

Reading Stimulus for Paket A: "Let's Take Social Media Breaks Seriously"

Thesis: It is time for teenagers to think again about how much time they spend on social media. Many students today check their phones more than one hundred times a day. This habit is quietly affecting their focus and their happiness.

Arguments: Firstly, a recent survey among high school students found that students who spend more than four hours a day on social media feel more stress and sleep less well. Comparing our lives to the curated posts of others can also hurt our self-esteem (how we feel about ourselves), even when we know these posts do not show the full picture. Secondly, experts explain that the constant notifications and endless scrolling train our brains to expect instant rewards. This makes it harder to focus on schoolwork or even enjoy a quiet moment without checking a screen.

Recommendation: Therefore, we should start taking regular social media breaks. Try turning off notifications during study time, set a daily time limit for social apps, and choose one hour before bed to be completely screen-free. Small breaks like these can protect both our mental health and our ability to focus on what really matters.

Reading Stimulus for Paket B: "It's Time to Move More, Sit Less"

Thesis: It is time for teenagers to think again about how much of their day they spend sitting still. They sit for long hours at school, during homework and in front of screens at home. Many students today are far less physically active than they realise.

Arguments: Firstly, a study by a national youth health organisation found that students who sit for more than eight hours a day have less energy and weaker concentration in class. Research also shows that regular physical activity, even in small amounts, improves mood and reduces stress. Secondly, health experts explain that our bodies are simply not designed for long periods of stillness. When an inactive lifestyle continues for years, muscles become weaker and the risk of long-term health problems increases.

Recommendation: Therefore, we should start moving more throughout the day. Take a short walk between study sessions, choose the stairs instead of the elevator when possible, and aim for at least thirty minutes of physical activity most days of the week. These small changes can improve both our energy and our long-term health.
