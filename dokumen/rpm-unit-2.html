MATERI PERTEMUAN 19: Mengenal Tokoh dan Tema Penemuan Jati Diri

A. What Is Self-Discovery in a Story?

Self-discovery means learning something true about who you really are. In fantasy stories, a character often feels unsure at the start of the story. By the end, after facing challenges, the character understands himself or herself better. This pattern is very common. Real people also experience it in everyday life, not only in stories.

Following a character's change: In a self-discovery story, we can follow the character's feelings from the beginning, to the middle, to the end. Ask two questions each time: How does the character feel about himself or herself? Which words in the story show it? Those words are our evidence.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 20: Struktur Teks, Elemen Cerita, Konflik dan Perubahan Tokoh

B. Text Structure: Four Parts

A story has four parts. Each part answers a question.

Part What It Does
Orientation (Who? Where? When?) It introduces the main character, the place and the time.
Complication (What goes wrong?) A problem appears. Often the biggest problem is inside the character, like fear or self-doubt.
Resolution (How is it solved?) The problem is solved, and we see the result. It often starts with a turning point: the character decides to change.
Re-orientation / Coda It closes the story: how the character changed or what they discovered. It can be optional in stories we read, but it is required in the group comic.

C. Story Elements

Element Meaning
Character The people or creatures in the story, especially the main character.
Setting The time and place where the story happens.
Conflict The main problem the character faces. It can be internal (inside the character, like fear or doubt) or external (outside, like a storm, a test or another character).
Resolution How the problem is solved, and what the character learns.

D. Two Kinds of Conflict and Character Change

Every story has a conflict, a problem the main character must deal with. There are two kinds. Look at where the problem comes from.

Kind of Conflict What It Means
Internal conflict A problem inside the character, like fear, doubt or a hard choice. Example: you want to study for tomorrow's exam, but you feel scared you will fail.
External conflict A problem from outside the character, like another person, an event or an obstacle. Example: your friend invites you to hang out the night before the exam.
Character change How the main character is different at the end of the story. To find it, compare the character at the beginning with the character at the end, and look for words that show the difference.

Important note: a story can have both kinds of conflict at the same time. In The Mirror of Selara, the internal conflict is Kirana's self-doubt, and the external conflict is the three trials from Selara. By the end, Kirana changes: she goes from feeling unsure and comparing herself to others to feeling quietly confident about who she is.

E. Model Text: "The Mirror of Selara"

Orientation: In a quiet village at the edge of an old forest, there lived a girl named Kirana. She was clever and kind, but she often felt unsure of who she really was, especially when she compared herself to her louder, more confident classmates. The villagers spoke of an ancient, magical mirror hidden deep in the forest. It was said to show anyone who found it their truest reflection.

Complication: One evening, curious and restless, Kirana followed a trail of glowing fireflies into the forest and found the mirror. The mirror was guarded by a gentle spirit named Selara. Then Selara told Kirana that to see her true reflection, she had to complete three trials: a trial of courage, a trial of honesty and a trial of kindness. Kirana doubted herself throughout each trial. She was certain she would fail, and twice she almost turned back, afraid that her true reflection would disappoint her.

Resolution: With Selara's quiet encouragement, Kirana first faced her fear during the trial of courage. Then came the trial of honesty. Kirana had to face a truth she had been avoiding. It was difficult, but she finally stopped hiding her feelings and admitted the truth to herself for the first time. She understood that being honest did not make her weak. After that, Kirana faced the trial of kindness. She found a small creature that was lost and frightened. Instead of thinking about herself, Kirana stopped to help it. She guided the creature back to safety with patience and care. When she finally looked into the mirror, Kirana did not see someone perfect. She saw herself clearly and honestly, with both flaws and strengths. Selara smiled and said, "The mirror never changes what it shows. It only waits until you are ready to truly look."

Re-orientation / Coda: The next morning, Kirana returned to her village with quiet confidence. From that day on, she no longer needed to compare herself to anyone else. She had finally learned that knowing who she truly was mattered more than being like someone else.

Identifikasi Murid

Pengetahuan Awal: Murid telah mengidentifikasi elemen cerita (tokoh, latar, konflik, resolusi) dan perubahan tokoh utama dari “The Mirror of Selara” (TP 2.1), namun belum terbiasa menganalisis unsur kebahasaan tingkat lanjut (noun phrase, adverbia, reduksi klausa adjektiva, reported speech) dan belum menguasai dasar klausa/frasa secara sistematis.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 21: Kosakata Makhluk Fantastis dan Well-Being

A. Vocabulary List

Word Definition Evidence from the Story
Spirit A magical being, often gentle or wise. "...a gentle spirit named Selara."
Guardian Someone who protects or watches over a place or a person. "The mirror was guarded by a gentle spirit named Selara." (inference: Selara guards the mirror)
Trial A difficult test that a character must pass, often to show something about themselves. "...to see her true reflection, she had to complete three trials..."
Reflection An image in a mirror; also, careful thought about yourself. "...to see her true reflection..."
Self-doubt A feeling of not being sure about your own abilities. "Kirana doubted herself throughout each trial." (inference: this shows her self-doubt)
Confidence A feeling of trust in your own abilities. "The next morning, Kirana returned to her village with quiet confidence."

Note on well-being: well-being means feeling healthy, happy and good about yourself. In The Mirror of Selara, Kirana's well-being grows as she moves from self-doubt to confidence. Fantasy words can help students talk about real feelings in their own lives.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 22: Noun Phrase dan Adverbia

B. Noun Phrases

A noun phrase is a group of words built around a noun. A simple pattern is: (article) + (adjective) + noun. Examples from the story: "a gentle spirit", "an ancient, magical mirror", "a quiet village", "glowing fireflies".

C. Adverbs of Place, Time, and Manner

Type Question It Answers Example from the Story
Place Where? "...deep in the forest..."
Time When? "One evening, curious and restless, Kirana followed..."
Manner How? "She saw herself clearly and honestly..." (clearly, honestly)

MATERI PERTEMUAN 23: Klausa, Reduksi Klausa Adjektiva, dan Reported Speech

D. Independent and Dependent Clauses

An independent clause has a subject and a verb, and it can stand alone as a complete sentence. Example: "Kirana doubted herself throughout each trial." A dependent clause also has a subject and a verb, but it cannot stand alone. It depends on another clause to make full sense. Example: "...when she compared herself to her louder, more confident classmates..." (this needs the rest of the sentence to make sense).

E. Reducing an Adjective Clause

An adjective clause that begins with "who/which/that + be verb" can often be shortened by removing the relative pronoun and the be verb. If the part that is left is a noun phrase, it is called an appositive (Selara, a gentle spirit). If the part that is left is an adjective phrase (full of light), it is not an appositive. It is simply a reduced adjective clause.

Full Adjective Clause Reduced Form
Selara, who was a gentle spirit, guided Kirana. Selara, a gentle spirit, guided Kirana. (appositive: a noun phrase is left)
Kirana found a mirror which was full of light. Kirana found a mirror full of light. (an adjective phrase is left: not an appositive)

F. Reported Speech (Basic)

When we report what someone said, we usually change the tense one step back into the past, and we may need to change pronouns too. Example: Direct speech: Selara said, "The mirror never changes what it shows." Reported speech: Selara said that the mirror never changed what it showed.

MATERI PERTEMUAN 24: Menulis Terintegrasi

G. Combining the Language Features

In Lesson 24, students write a short story (6-8 sentences) about a moment of self-discovery: their own, someone they know, or a made-up character (students choose). The story has four parts (Orientation, Complication, Resolution, Coda) and uses noun phrases, adverbs, at least one clause or reduced adjective clause, and at least one reported speech sentence.

Model paragraph (for teacher demonstration only): "One day, I felt unsure about a difficult decision. My best friend, a very honest person, told me that I should trust myself. Slowly, I found the confidence to make my own choice." Notice the appositive noun phrase ("a very honest person", reduced from "who was a very honest person"), the adverbs of time and manner ("one day", "slowly"), the reported speech ("told me that I should trust myself"), and the simple past tense used throughout.

LAMPIRAN 4 - MATERI AJAR (IN ENGLISH, LENGKAP)

A. What Makes a Good Story Planner?

A story planner is a simple document. It shows all the important decisions about a story before production begins. A good story planner saves time later, because the group agrees on the plot, the characters and the roles before anyone starts drawing or writing dialogue.

B. Four-Part Structure for the Motion Comic Project

Part What It Shows
1. Orientation Introduces the main character and their normal life before the self-discovery journey begins.
2. Complication Shows the challenge, doubt, or problem that pushes the character to change.
3. Resolution Starts with the turning point: the moment the character decides to change. Then it shows how the problem is solved and what the character learns.
4. Coda Shows what the character discovered about themselves and how they have changed. This part is required.

C. Responsible Use of AI Tools

Groups may use AI tools (such as Canva AI or an image generator) to help create illustrations, characters or backgrounds. But the story idea, the plot and the dialogue must be the group's own work. AI is a tool to support your creativity. It does not replace it.

D. Story Meets Science: The Design Cycle

A story works like a design cycle in science. Orientation shows the problem. Complication shows the first attempt, and it fails. Resolution starts with the turning point: a new idea or a brave choice. Then the character improves the idea and solves the problem. Coda is the conclusion: what the character discovered about themselves. In a lab report, the data tells us what happened. In a narrative, the character shows who they became.

E. From Comic to Motion Comic

A motion comic is a digital comic with recorded voices. Each panel has a short narration in the simple past and dialogue in direct speech. Before recording, the group writes a panel script by hand. Every member writes the narration for at least one part and records at least one voice part. The handwritten script shows that the ideas and dialogue are your own. The final video is 2-3 minutes long.

LAMPIRAN 5 - MATERI AJAR: REKAMAN DAN PRESENTASI MOTION COMIC (IN ENGLISH)

A. Recording Techniques for a Motion Comic

- Record in a quiet place. Hold the phone about 20 cm from your mouth.

- Speak a little slower than normal, and change your voice a little for each character.

- Record one panel at a time. If you make a mistake, just record that panel again.

- Stop for a moment after an important part of the story. This gives listeners time to feel it.

B. Presentation Sentence Frames

- Live opening: "Good morning, everyone. Our motion comic is called... It is about..."

- After the video: "We chose this story because..."

- Coda and closing: "Our character discovered that... Thank you for watching. Do you have any questions?"

Tip: if a listener asks a question that you do not understand, you can say: "Can you repeat the question, please?" This is a normal and polite question. Good speakers use it too.

C. Making the Motion Comic (2-3 Minutes)

- Put the panels in order: Orientation, Complication, Resolution, Coda.

- Add the recording for each panel. Keep each panel on the screen until its recording ends.

- Add a title at the start. At the end, add a credits slide with every member's name and job (who wrote and who recorded each part).

- Export the video (MP4) and upload it to the class Drive folder.

D. Our Class E-book

- One page for each group: the title, two or three key panels, a QR code or link to the video and each member's Coda paragraph.

Reading Text for Paket B: "The Song of the Silver Owl"

(1) In a small town in the mountains, there lived a girl named Larasati. She loved music and had a beautiful voice, but she always felt unsure about it. The leader of the town choir was famous because she sang very well. Larasati often said to herself, "My voice is not good enough." The people of the town told a story about a silver owl in the old bell tower. They said the owl sang back only to someone who sang from an honest heart.

(2) One foggy morning, Larasati climbed the long stairs of the old bell tower and found the silver owl. The owl's name was Wulan, and she guarded the tower day and night. Wulan said, "To hear my song, you must complete three challenges: a challenge of honesty, a challenge of practice and a challenge of singing in front of others." During each challenge, Larasati compared herself to the choir leader, and twice she almost stopped singing. But she kept going.

(3) In the last challenge, the whole town gathered in front of the tower to listen, and the choir leader stood in the front row. Larasati's voice shook, but she did not stop. She closed her eyes and sang her own song to the end. When she finished the last note, the silver owl finally sang back with a clear, beautiful sound. Wulan smiled and said, "The owl was never listening for a perfect voice. It was only waiting for a voice that was truly yours."

(4) Larasati walked down from the tower with quiet pride. She no longer needed to sound like anyone else.
